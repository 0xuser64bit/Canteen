# Campus launch playbook

Launch on one or two campuses where a founder or a real volunteer can spend time answering questions. Keep the wider network available, but direct invitations to the campus experience. Do not manufacture posts, accounts, votes, testimonials or activity counts.

## Before inviting students

- Assign a real owner for each campus and an available moderator. Agree on the hours they can cover; do not promise round-the-clock responses.
- Verify university sign-in, campus isolation, account deletion, private image delivery, moderation and appeal review on the deployment. Configure scheduled maintenance and monitor the oldest overdue deletion job. Finish legacy public-image migration before describing all historical photos as private.
- Publish the community/privacy page and check that its storage and backup statements match the hosting setup. Give previews an isolated database.
- Have real campus volunteers transparently introduce their role. For staff hosts, explicitly assign campus domains with `CAMPUS_HOSTS` and use labeled Canteen team accounts; merely adding `TEAM_EMAILS` does not grant private campus discussion access. Review the campus-only access disclosure before enabling assignments. Do not present paid or staff activity as independent student enthusiasm.

## First two weeks

1. Invite a small group personally, with a direct campus link and a concrete reason to use it: an upcoming course choice, housing question or internship deadline. Ask permission before adding anyone to outreach lists.
2. Ask hosts to share real questions, useful answers and current opportunities with official application links. A founder can post an honest introduction from a team account. Empty states should remain honest until students participate.
3. Check `/host` for assigned campuses or “Needs an answer” during agreed coverage windows. Start with oldest waiting; use “No replies yet” to distinguish neglected questions from active discussions. Answer only where the host has useful knowledge, invite a relevant volunteer with consent, and acknowledge uncertainty. Never mark an answer helpful on the author's behalf.
4. Set `OPS_ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL` to a channel the moderator watches: at-risk reports, content hidden by reports, new appeals and failed maintenance page it, and at-risk reports sort first in `/mod`. Still check reports and appeals daily during the pilot. Review context and choose the reason shown to the author. Keep reporter identities out of support conversations.
5. Ask a few consenting students what stopped them from posting, which answer helped, and what made them return. Keep personal interview notes outside public threads; do not export account-linked discussion histories.

## Weekly decision

Use the team-only `/launch` page. Its primary usefulness measure is distinct question authors who selected a helpful reply in the past seven UTC days. Look alongside weekly active students and week-two return among cohorts whose full observation window has elapsed. Low-volume cells are suppressed, so early qualitative conversations matter. These are operational signals, not causal proof or a promise of growth.

Compare each campus with its own previous weeks. Write down one observation, one change to try, its owner, and when to review it. Fix recurring unanswered needs and moderation delays before inviting another campus. Set expansion targets after a baseline exists; do not invent success numbers before real usage.

## Operational checks

Monitor failed sign-ins, publishing errors, moderation/appeal backlog and the oldest overdue `BlobDeletion`. The daily maintenance endpoint retries up to four batches of ten deletion jobs within its time budget and migrates one legacy image per invocation; immediate deletion actions also trigger a batch. Drain larger backlogs by invoking the authenticated endpoint repeatedly, or increase the deployment's schedule within its plan limits. Confirm actual erasure in storage before declaring a migration complete. Daily activity, publication limiter, domain-request and auth rate-limit retention also depend on maintenance running. `/launch` shows heartbeat timestamps, deletion backlog/retries and oldest unresolved reports/appeals. Failed runs page `OPS_ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL`; a run that never starts can't page anyone, so also configure an external check for a successful maintenance run within 26 hours, and investigate any growing overdue backlog. Set escalation ownership and response targets for the actual pilot.

Never put email addresses, account IDs, tokens or post text into analytics dashboards or routine error logs. Database operators still have privileged access to account-linked records; application aliases are not database anonymity. Track service health without exporting student content.

## Deployment and migration sequence

1. Choose a real campus owner, moderator and coverage hours. Keep `CAMPUS_HOSTS` empty until the owner accepts the disclosed access model. For each assigned host, put the email in `TEAM_EMAILS`, ensure the account belongs to the Canteen team university, and map only approved canonical primary domains. Removing an assignment revokes normal campus reads, posts and reply alerts on the next request; removal from `TEAM_EMAILS` ends that account's access entirely on its next request, including moderation and posting as "Canteen team".
2. Provision an isolated preview database and private test Blob store. Set preview-scoped auth/email/Blob variables. Use synthetic accounts and mocked delivery in CI. Explicitly migrate that database before the preview build: `DATABASE_URL="$PREVIEW_DATABASE_URL" DATABASE_URL_UNPOOLED="$PREVIEW_DATABASE_URL" bun run db:deploy`. Both URLs must identify the same isolated preview database. Never point a preview at production.
3. Run `bun run check`, then the full database suite against disposable native PostgreSQL using the README commands. Real multi-connection PostgreSQL is required to verify row/advisory locks; a single-backend embedded engine validates SQL and sequential behavior only. Verify an ordinary university sign-in, a personal staff OTP, fresh/returning Google sign-in when enabled, and malicious return destinations. Confirm new Account rows have null provider-token fields without inspecting or exporting tokens.
4. Test two accounts sharing one browser and a second tab: draft recovery, failed submission, repeated submission ID, sign-out/deletion, session switch, and blocked storage. Test a long thread's earlier page while replies/likes/moderation arrive, poll changes from another account, failed-send recovery while typing, more than 50 alerts, and accessible image descriptions on keyboard and screen-reader paths.
5. Back up the deployment database using the provider's approved process. Review the token cleanup and recent-publication backfill migrations, including their lock/write cost on the actual row counts. Apply the six ordered audit migrations before serving the new code. Production `vercel-build` applies them automatically using the configured direct migration connection. Existing provider tokens are cleared permanently; sign-in binding fields are retained. Do not rotate `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET` as part of this rollout: it also derives aliases.
6. Verify private image reads reject outsiders, post/account deletion erases text immediately, and storage failures leave durable deletion work. Exercise legacy migration racing deletion on disposable storage. Keep the legacy token until public originals are erased and the backlog is empty. Do not declare historical images private or erased based on a successful HTTP response alone; confirm the provider's object state.
7. Configure `CRON_SECRET` and daily maintenance (or a faster schedule supported by your host). Invoke the authenticated maintenance route, inspect `/launch`, and confirm success timestamps advance, overdue work drains, and retention removes expired fixture rows. Test a failure and alert delivery to the real operational owner. Monitor aggregate provider sign-in failures and generic publishing failures without exporting email addresses, account IDs, tokens, IPs or discussion text.
8. Review `/university-request` submissions from `/launch`. Independently verify the official university and domain, update `data/universities.json`, test domain mapping and deploy, then mark the request resolved. Dismiss unsafe or unverifiable submissions. Do not accept a domain merely because a request exists, and do not send unsolicited follow-up messages.

For rollback, revert application code while retaining the additive tables/columns until compatibility is assessed. Do not recreate discarded provider tokens or drop deletion intents. A failed migration must be diagnosed before changing Prisma migration history; never reset a production database.
